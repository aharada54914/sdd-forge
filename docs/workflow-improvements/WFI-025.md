## Problem Evidence

A task-stage provenance binding made over a status-mixed `tasks.md` breaks on
the very next lifecycle transition, and there is no validator-legal way to make
one that does not.

`check-workflow-state.sh` accepts four canonical forms of the task-plan hash:

1. `normalized_hash` — every `Status:` rewritten to `Planned`, every
   `Approval:` to the draft value
2. the raw bytes
3. `rereview_normalized_hash(..., "Implementation Complete")`
4. `rereview_normalized_hash(..., "Done")`

Forms 1, 3 and 4 are invariant under status transitions: each rewrites every
`Status:`, `Approval:` and review-status line to a constant before hashing, so
any change confined to those lines leaves the digest untouched. Form 2 is not
invariant.

A recorded raw digest therefore survives a lifecycle flip only when
normalization was already a no-op at the moment it was taken — that is, when the
plan sat in one of the uniform canonical states, so its raw bytes coincided with
form 3 or form 4 and the binding inherited their invariance. A plan whose tasks
are in mixed states — the normal condition for any epic mid-flight — has a raw
digest that coincides with none of the three invariant forms. The binding is
fragile by construction, and any subsequent flip of any task invalidates it.

That would be a tolerable convention if a durable binding were reachable. It is
not:

- `validate-review-context-set.sh` requires every manifest entry's declared
  `sha256` to equal the live file's raw sha256.
- The round-consistency check requires the manifest to agree with the
  precheck's `tasks_sha256`.
- `task-review-precheck.sh` computes `tasks_sha256` as the raw hash (line 356
  as of the `lib/review-precheck-common.sh` refactor; line 494 at drafting
  time).

The only binding the sanctioned reservation path can produce is therefore the
one form that is guaranteed to break.

Observed directly, three times in one session, on three separate features:

- `epic-190-a2`: rebound to attempt-5 after T-007 reached Done — commit
  `340f0149`, artifact
  `reports/task-review/epic-190-a2-capability-registry/attempt-5/round-1/precheck-result.json`.
- `epic-194-a6`: rebound to attempt-3 — commit `b836a11e`, artifact
  `reports/task-review/epic-194-a6-lite-integration/attempt-3/round-1/precheck-result.json`.
  The attempt-2 binding recorded raw bytes while T-004 was `Implementation
  Complete`, and the quality gate's flip to `Done` — the only byte change to
  `tasks.md` since — broke it. Both reviewers independently raised that the
  replacement binding is mixed too and will need redoing.
- `epic-191-a3`: rebound to attempt-5 after T-002 and T-005 reached Done —
  commit `726a5a0c`, artifact
  `reports/task-review/epic-191-a3-path-ownership/attempt-5/round-1/precheck-result.json`.

These are primary artifacts rather than retrospective-table rows:
`reports/retrospective/2026-08-14T034801Z-pillar-a-wave-session.md` now covers
this session and records this mechanism as FP-06 (Confidence: High), but the
retrospective schema has no column for rebind counts today, so the metric still
cannot be read from a retrospective table. Item 5 of `## Verification Plan` adds
that column so the next cycle can read the metric from the usual place.

Demonstrated on a scratch copy of `epic-191-a3`'s `tasks.md` (the real file
untouched; the copy carried 2 `Done`, 2 `Implementation Complete` and 2
`Planned`). Flipping a single task to `Done` changes the raw digest
(`7bf1a5cd…` → `4342957c…`), while the normalized digest
(`ff0a6b45…`) and both rereview-normalized digests (`42f23eff…`, `82b42b0a…`)
come out **byte-identical before and after** the same flip. Replaying the
gate's own `reviewed_hash_accepted` against the flipped copy then rejects the
pre-flip raw binding and accepts the pre-flip normalized binding — both halves
of the problem, exercised against the real acceptance code.

Each rebind costs two reviewer launches, two ledger reservations and a
full contract artifact set — for a file whose semantic content did not change.

## Why-Why Analysis

| # | Why | Because | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why did the task-stage provenance binding break three times in one session? | Each binding recorded the raw digest of a mixed-status task plan, and the next lifecycle flip of any task changed the raw bytes, invalidating the binding. | commits 340f0149, b836a11e, 726a5a0c; the three `precheck-result.json` artifacts cited in `## Problem Evidence` |
| 2 | Why did each binding record the fragile raw digest instead of a status-invariant form? | The producing side computes the plan digest as the raw hash, and the reservation validator requires every manifest entry to equal the live file's raw sha256 and to agree with that recorded value, so the sanctioned path can produce no other form. | `task-review-precheck.sh:356` (formerly :494, moved by the `lib/review-precheck-common.sh` refactor); `validate-review-context-set.sh:460-462, 465-499` |
| 3 | Why can the producer not choose one of the durable forms the accepting side already admits? | The invariant forms exist only in the accepting side's check; no producing-side script or authoring instruction computes or records a normalized digest, so on a mixed-status plan the raw digest coincides with none of the invariant forms. | `check-workflow-state.sh:250-260`; the STEP 2/STEP 4 "with current hashes" instruction at SKILL.md:89,139 |
| 4 | Why does this production/acceptance asymmetry exist? | The extra accepted forms were added to the accepting side for the re-review path, where the plan is uniform and the raw bytes coincide with an invariant form by accident of state; the producing side was never updated to cover the ordinary mid-flight case. | (hypothesis) — consistent with the design comment above `rereview_normalized_hash` in `check-workflow-state.sh:224-239` |

The chain terminates at a controllable mechanism — durable digest forms added
to the accepting side without a corresponding producing-side change — which is
what `## Proposed Change` acts on.

## Root Cause Hypothesis

The four accepted forms were designed to make the binding survive lifecycle
transitions, and three of them do. The *producing* side — precheck and
validator — emits only raw bytes.

That is sufficient for the re-review path the extra forms were written for,
because a re-review runs against a uniform plan: there the raw bytes coincide
with form 3 or form 4, and the binding silently inherits their invariance. It is
not sufficient for the ordinary mid-flight case. On a mixed plan the raw digest
coincides with nothing, so the accepting side is more permissive than the
producing side can ever exercise, and the durable forms are unreachable for the
one state that actually needs them — reachable otherwise only by hand-authoring
a manifest, which the validator then rejects for other reasons.

The invariance of the accepted forms is therefore real but accidental in
practice: it is a property of the file's state when the digest was taken, not
something the producer can choose. This is consistent with the extra forms
having been added to the accepting side without a corresponding change to the
producing side.

## Proposed Change

Make the producing side able to emit a durable binding for the case that needs
it.

| Target File | Change Description |
|---|---|
| `plugins/sdd-review-loop/scripts/task-review-precheck.sh` and its `.ps1` peer | When the task plan's statuses are **not** uniform, compute the recorded plan digest as the normalized digest rather than the raw one, and record which form was used under a new `tasks_sha256_form` field (`raw` \| `normalized`). A uniform plan keeps today's raw behaviour byte-for-byte. |
| `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/validate-review-context-set.sh` and its `.ps1` peer | Allow the task plan's manifest entry to declare the normalized digest **only** when the precheck result for the same attempt and round declares `tasks_sha256_form: normalized`. Every other manifest entry keeps the current strict raw-digest requirement, so the exception is scoped to the one file whose status lines the normalization is defined over. |
| `plugins/sdd-review-loop/skills/task-review-loop/SKILL.md` | STEP 2 and STEP 4 currently tell the launching agent to build the reviewer's allowed-input manifest "with current hashes", which for the task plan means a fresh raw digest of the live file. Change that instruction: read the round's precheck result first, and when it declares `tasks_sha256_form: normalized`, copy its recorded plan digest verbatim into the manifest entry instead of recomputing. Otherwise recompute the raw digest as today. |
| `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/check-workflow-state.sh` | No change — the accepting side already admits the normalized form. Listed so the audit can see the full blast radius: two scripts plus their PowerShell peers, and one authoring instruction. |

The scope is deliberately narrow. It does not weaken hash binding — a
normalized digest still binds every byte of the plan except the status and
approval fields, which are exactly the fields the lifecycle is *supposed* to
change. It does not touch the identity ledger or the reservation protocol.

**Why the authoring instruction is in scope.** The validator consumes a manifest
supplied as its first argument; it does not build one. No script in the
repository constructs `allowed_input_manifest` — the launching agent assembles it
from the SKILL's prose. So changing only the two scripts would leave the manifest
still declaring a raw digest while the precheck recorded a normalized one, and
the round-consistency check would fail every mid-flight reservation closed: the
exact inverse of the intended effect. Only the task decomposition review gate's
reviewer manifest carries a task-plan entry; the specification and design
review gates do not manifest `tasks.md`, so no equivalent change is needed
there.

**Non-decreasing guard (`Meta-Change: true`).** The change removes no gate,
check or test. It adds one conditional on the producing side, one cross-check
in the validator that does not exist today (the `tasks_sha256_form` agreement
between manifest and precheck result), and the five tests below. The accepting
side's four-form check is untouched. The count of deterministic checks strictly
increases.

**Why this is not a loosened gate.** The normalized form is already accepted
today; the change does not widen what the gate admits by even one digest. It
moves which of the already-accepted forms the producer is able to emit, and
gates that choice on a recorded, cross-checked field so a normalized binding
cannot be claimed for a plan the precheck saw as uniform.

## Expected Effect

A task-stage binding made mid-flight survives every subsequent status
transition of that feature, so a rebind is needed only when the plan's
substance changes — not when the workflow does its job.

Concretely, each of the three observed features consumed at least one
additional task decomposition review gate attempt solely to re-bind after a
status flip (the rebinds that produced attempts 5, 3 and 5). A durable binding
removes that class of attempt entirely: rebinds attributable solely to a
status transition fall from an average of 1.0 per feature to 0.

## Verification Plan

1. A new test in `tests/task-review-precheck.tests.{sh,ps1}`: build a fixture
   plan with mixed statuses, run the precheck, assert `tasks_sha256_form` is
   `normalized` and the hash equals the normalized hash. Build a uniform plan,
   assert the form is `raw`.
2. A non-vacuity mutation for each: flip the fixture's mixture and assert the
   emitted form changes. A test that passes under both shapes proves nothing.
3. An end-to-end test over the whole reservation path, not just the two
   scripts: run the precheck on a mixed-status plan, build the manifest the way
   the revised SKILL instruction directs, reserve it through
   `validate-review-context-set.sh`, then flip one task's status and assert
   `check-workflow-state.sh` still exits 0 — the property this WFI exists to
   create. Assert the same sequence fails before the change, so the test is
   known to discriminate. Assert also that a manifest built the *old* way (a
   fresh raw digest) against a normalized precheck result is rejected at
   reservation rather than silently accepted, so the two halves cannot drift
   apart unnoticed.
4. Confirm the scoped exception cannot be used on any other file: attempt a
   manifest declaring a normalized hash for a non-`tasks.md` entry and assert
   the validator rejects it. Assert the same for a `tasks.md` entry whose
   round's precheck result declares `tasks_sha256_form: raw`, so the
   cross-check itself is shown to bind.
5. The next retrospective adds a `task-stage rebinds attributable to a status
   transition` row to its Improvement Verification Plan table, populated per
   feature over the measurement window and compared against this WFI's baseline
   (average 1.0 per feature) and target (0). Without that row the metric above
   cannot be read from the retrospective in the next cycle.

## Rollback-Plan

Revert the precheck and validator changes. The producing side returns to
raw-only and `tasks_sha256_form` becomes inert; because the accepting side was
never modified, nothing needs to be un-widened there.

Bindings already recorded in normalized form would then fail the validator's
raw-digest requirement on their next reservation, and each affected feature
would need one rebind — which is the current steady state anyway, so the
rollback cost is bounded by the problem this WFI exists to remove. No data
migration, no ledger surgery, no change to already-passed gates.

## Result

**Applied 2026-08-22** (commit whose message contains `WFI-025`). All four
Proposed Change rows landed as proposed, with the implementation details the
table left open resolved as follows:

- **Producing side** (`task-review-precheck.{sh,ps1}`): uniformity = every
  `^Status:` line carries one value (no `Status:` lines counts as uniform);
  a uniform plan's digest and output are byte-for-byte unchanged. The
  normalized recipe replicates `check-workflow-state`'s task normalization
  exactly in each twin (sh↔sh, ps1↔ps1, CR handling included). The
  precheck's `--verify-inputs` / `-VerifyInputs` mode also honors the
  declared form, so a pure status flip between precheck and launch no
  longer fails input verification while a body edit still does.
- **Validator twins** (`validate-review-context-set.{sh,ps1}`): the scoped
  exception fires only for `specs/<feature>/tasks.md` on `stage: task`,
  and requires all three of: the round's precheck declares
  `tasks_sha256_form: normalized`; the manifest hash equals the precheck's
  recorded digest; and the LIVE file re-normalizes to that digest — the
  entry still binds every byte outside the lifecycle fields, re-derived
  from disk rather than trusted from the record. Every other entry keeps
  the strict raw requirement. The old-way manifest (a fresh raw digest
  against a normalized precheck) is rejected by the existing
  round-consistency check with no new code.
- **Authoring instruction** (`task-review-loop/SKILL.md` STEP 2 and STEP
  4): the `tasks.md` entry is documented as the one exception to "current
  hashes" — copy the precheck's recorded digest verbatim when the form is
  normalized; recompute raw otherwise.
- **Verification Plan items 1-4** are implemented in
  `tests/task-plan-binding-durability.tests.sh` (registered in run-all and
  the CI matrix; 16 passed, 0 failed): P1 runs the real precheck on a
  uniform then mixed plan (form and digest asserted against the accepting
  side's recipe, non-vacuous by the flip); P2 exercises the reservation
  validator on isolated ledger fixtures — normalized accepted, status flip
  absorbed, body edit refused, fresh-raw refused at reservation,
  raw-form-precheck misuse refused, non-tasks.md misuse refused; P3 runs
  the UNCHANGED `check-workflow-state.sh` on a mid-flight rebind of the
  workflow-state-integrity fixture: the normalized binding survives a
  lifecycle flip while the raw binding — the only form the pre-WFI-025
  precheck could emit — breaks on the same flip. That raw control is the
  measured before/after the plan's discriminating requirement asks for.
  A subtlety the control surfaced, worth recording: on a COMPLETED
  (uniformly-Done) plan a raw binding accidentally survives, because the
  raw bytes coincide with the accepting side's re-review form — precisely
  the accidental invariance `## Root Cause Hypothesis` describes; the
  fixture therefore rebinds from a mixed mid-flight state.
