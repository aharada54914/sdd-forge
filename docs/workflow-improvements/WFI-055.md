## Problem Evidence

A suite can be registered in `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}`, pass locally, be cited
as a change's verification, and **never run in CI**. Nothing keeps the CI
workflow's step list in agreement with the runner's, and nothing records which
omissions are deliberate.

Measured on `bcdb6381`:

| | |
|---|---|
| `*.tests.sh` suites registered in `tests/run-all.sh` | **117** |
| of those, not named anywhere in `.github/workflows/test.yml` | **48** |
| times `.github/workflows/test.yml` invokes `run-all` | **0** |

The workflow mentions `run-all` three times. All three are comments, and two
describe this very defect after hitting it:

> *"eight suites were left unregistered here while already registered in
> `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}`"* — `.github/workflows/test.yml:548`, epic-189-a1 T-013

> *"All four suites are registered in the live `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}`; this
> staged candidate supplies their previously missing direct CI steps."*
> — `:778`, epic-191-a3

Both were fixed the same way: by hand, for the suites one epic happened to
notice, adding nothing that would catch the next one. There is no documented
allowlist of intentional exclusions either — `git grep` across `docs/`,
`tests/` and `.github/` for "not registered in CI", "deliberately
unregistered", "intentionally omitted" and equivalents returns nothing on this
subject — so a reader cannot tell an omission from a decision.

### One comparison does exist, and it is the sharpest evidence here

`tests/deterministic-lane-selfcheck.tests.sh:267-272` already asserts that a CI
step exists for a suite:

```
=== TEST-020 (AC-020): CI step per new suite, DESIGNED-RED until human-copy ===
    grep -Fq "bash ./${suite}" "$candidate"
    -> fail "TEST-020: the candidate has no CI step running ${suite}"
```

So the idea is not novel, and an earlier feature reached for it. Two things
limit it, and the second is the point of this ticket:

1. It iterates `$NEW_SUITES` — the four suites that one epic is adding — not
   `run-all`'s inventory. It cannot see the 44 omissions outside that set, which
   is why the existing check covers only part of the 48.
2. **`deterministic-lane-selfcheck.tests.sh` is itself one of the 48.** The
   suite that checks whether suites have CI steps does not run in CI.

WFI-045 (`Status: Draft`) independently reached the same conclusion about the
allowlist half, writing of the case "where an existing suite must carry an
opt-out marker for a deliberate omission" (`:318`). Two tickets and one suite
have now each built or proposed a piece of this, none of them enforced.

### What it cost, measured on this change

**This was not inferred. It was performed**, during the WFI-048 apply on
2026-08-26.

Five human-copy mirrors stage copies of the same repository-wide guard files:

| Mirror | Stages |
|---|---|
| `specs/epic-136-phase2-gates/human-copy` | guard twins, `guard-parity.tests.sh`, `lite-spec` |
| `specs/epic-189-a1-project-context/human-copy` | invariants, guard twins, `lite-spec`, `ship` |
| `specs/epic-191-a3-path-ownership/human-copy` | invariants |
| `specs/epic-190-a2-capability-registry/human-copy` | invariants |
| `specs/epic-190-a2-capability-registry/drafts/human-copy-candidate` | invariants |

Across the three manifest-bearing bundles: 41 entries, **29 distinct paths, 12
of them staged by more than one bundle**.

CI surfaced them **one round at a time** — `phase2` and `epic-189-a1` in the
first cycle, `epic-191-a3` and a `ship-track-selection` publication-pair check
in the second. The two `epic-190-a2` mirrors were **never named by CI at all**
and would have shipped stale.

`tests/human-copy-mirror-freshness.tests.sh` exists precisely to prevent that.
WFI-039 built it, and its own header says so:

> *"Each existing suite enforces its own bundle and is blind to the others, so
> editing a shared file surfaces the mirrors one CI round at a time — three
> rounds on PR #305, one mirror each. … It adds the one thing none of them can
> do: enumerate EVERY mirror and report EVERY stale one in a single failure."*

Run locally it did exactly that: one failure naming all eleven stale entries,
including the two `epic-190-a2` mirrors nothing else would have caught. WFI-039
also shipped `scripts/sync-human-copy-mirrors.py`, which fixed them in one
command and correctly left the 14 `pending` mirrors alone.

Registration, measured:

| | `run-all.sh` | `run-all.ps1` | `.github/workflows/test.yml` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `human-copy-mirror-freshness.tests.sh` | 1 | 1 | **0** |
| `guard-parity.tests.sh` | 1 | 1 | **0** |

The second row is the sharper one. `guard-parity.tests.sh` is the suite WFI-048
extended with 31 new cases and cited as its verification artifact. Those cases
do not run in CI. The change was reviewed, merged and applied on the strength
of a green suite the pipeline never executes.

### The gap is in the convention, not in one author's diligence

WFI-039 is `Status: Applied`, and it was applied exactly as written. Its
`## Proposed Change` table lists two targets: the new suite, and
`tests/run-all.sh` and `tests/run-all.ps1` to *"Register the new suite."* The
CI workflow is not among them.

So this is not a proposal that was half-applied. A WFI whose entire purpose was
to make a cross-cutting check exist treated `run-all` registration as
registration, because that is what the repository's convention means by the
word. The convention is the defect.

### Cost

WFI-039 solved this problem. The solution sat unregistered, so the defect it
was built to prevent recurred in full: three CI cycles to surface three
mirrors, two more never surfaced, and a verification suite that reports green
locally and is absent from the pipeline.

## Why-Why Analysis

| # | Why (question) | Because (answer) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why did five stale mirrors surface across three CI cycles instead of one failure? | The suite that reports all of them at once never ran. | `human-copy-mirror-freshness` in `run-all` ×2, in `test.yml` ×0 |
| 2 | Why did it not run? | CI enumerates suites individually and never invokes `run-all`, so registering there does nothing for CI. | 0 invocations; all three `run-all` mentions are comments |
| 3 | Why was it registered only in `run-all`? | That is what "register the suite" means in this repository. WFI-039's own Proposed Change names `run-all.{sh,ps1}` and stops. | `WFI-039.md`, `## Proposed Change`, target 2 |
| 4 | Why is the CI list allowed to diverge from the runner's? | The only comparison that exists is scoped to one epic's new suites, and the suite performing it is itself unregistered in CI. Registration is otherwise manual and per-epic. | `deterministic-lane-selfcheck.tests.sh:267-272` iterates `$NEW_SUITES`; that suite is one of the 48; two in-file comments record earlier manual reconciliations |
| 5 | Why does the divergence stay invisible? | A suite in `run-all` looks registered from the repository's side, passes locally, and no allowlist distinguishes a deliberate omission from an oversight. | `git grep` for exclusion wording returns nothing |

The chain ends at one controllable mechanism: **no check compares the runner's
FULL suite list with the CI workflow's, and the partial check that exists is
not itself run by CI.**

## Root Cause Hypothesis

The repository keeps two suite inventories — `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}` and
`.github/workflows/test.yml` — that must agree, and nothing that runs makes
them agree. A per-epic comparison exists and is unenforced, which is the same
outcome as none while being harder to notice: a reader who finds TEST-020
reasonably concludes the problem is handled.

`run-all` is the one authors edit, because it is the one they run. `test.yml`
is protected, so adding a step requires a staged patch and a human apply: a
separate action, easy to defer, easy to forget, and easy to believe unnecessary
when the suite already appears "registered". The two drift in one direction —
suites accumulate in `run-all` and are missing from CI.

The failure is silent in the worst way. The suite exists, it is registered, it
passes, and an author can cite it in good faith as verification — as WFI-048
did — while the pipeline never executes it. Neither file points at the other.

## Proposed Change

| Target File | Change Description |
|---|---|
| `.github/workflows/test.yml` | Register `tests/human-copy-mirror-freshness.tests.{sh,ps1}` and `tests/guard-parity.tests.{sh,ps1}` as direct steps. These two suites (four twin files) are named because this change measured their absence and its cost; the other 46 suites are addressed by the check below rather than by a blind bulk registration. Protected file, so a staged patch under `docs/ci-staging/`. |
| `tests/ci-suite-registration.tests.sh` and its `.ps1` twin (new) | Generalise what `deterministic-lane-selfcheck.tests.sh`'s TEST-020 already does for one epic's new suites: parse the suite list out of `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}` and out of `.github/workflows/test.yml` and fail on any suite present in the former and absent from the latter. Deliberate omissions are declared in an explicit allowlist file with a one-line reason each, so an omission is a recorded decision rather than an absence. The check also fails on an allowlist entry naming a suite that no longer exists, so the list cannot rot. |
| `tests/run-all.{sh,ps1}` | Register the new check — **and** register it in `.github/workflows/test.yml` in the same change. A registration check that is itself unregistered would reproduce this defect exactly. |
| `docs/ci-staging/README.md` | State that adding a suite to `run-all` is half the registration, and that the CI step is a staged patch a human must apply. Today that coupling is recorded only in two comments inside the workflow, where an author adding a suite will not look. |

**Not proposed.** Bulk-registering all 48 in one change. Some are plausibly
slow, platform-specific, or already covered by a step under a different name,
and this WFI measured the *list*, not each suite's fitness for the pipeline.
Registering them blind would trade a silent gap for a red pipeline and an
incentive to delete steps. The allowlist forces each of the 48 to be decided
once, with a reason, which is the outcome this WFI actually wants.

**Also not proposed.** Making CI call `run-all` directly. It would close the gap
in one line and discard the per-suite step granularity the workflow uses for
naming, ordering, timeouts and per-OS gating — a large architectural change to
win a property a comparison already gives.

**Recorded, not fixed here.** `phase2-guard-invariants.tests.ps1` asserts the
exact generated-invariant key set; the `.sh` twin has no equivalent assertion,
which is why the WFI-048 key-set failure appeared only on Windows. That is a
twin-parity defect rather than a registration defect and belongs in its own
ticket.

## Expected Effect

A suite added to `run-all` cannot silently miss CI. The next author who edits a
repository-wide file gets every stale mirror in one failure instead of one per
cycle, and a change cannot cite a suite as its verification while the pipeline
skips it.

Concretely: on the WFI-048 apply this would have replaced three CI cycles with
one and named the two `epic-190-a2` mirrors that no CI check reported at all.

## Verification Metric

Target-Metric: number of `*.tests.sh` suites registered in `tests/run-all.sh`
that are neither named in `.github/workflows/test.yml` nor listed in the
declared-omission allowlist
Expected-Direction: decrease
Horizon: at application
Baseline: 48 of 117 unregistered, 0 declared (measured 2026-08-26 on
`bcdb6381`; no allowlist exists)
Target: 0 undeclared. The count of *registered* suites is deliberately not the
metric — a suite moved onto the allowlist with a reason is a resolved case, and
a metric rewarding registration alone would push all 48 into the pipeline
regardless of fitness.

Secondary (the check is not vacuous): removing any one registered suite's step
from a scratch copy of the workflow must make the new check fail, and naming a
non-existent suite in the allowlist must also make it fail. Baseline: no check
exists. Target: both mutations detected.

Secondary (no pipeline regression): the suites registered by this change pass
in CI on all three runners. Baseline: not executed. Target: green on ubuntu,
macos and windows.

## Verification Plan

1. Record the baseline by parsing both inventories rather than reading them:
   the 48/117 figure above is reproducible and is what the new check computes.
2. Assert the defect before the change: `human-copy-mirror-freshness` and
   `guard-parity` are absent from `test.yml` while present in `run-all`. These
   are the assertions the fix must flip.
3. Assert the fix: both appear as direct CI steps and execute on all three
   runners.
4. Mutation, in **both** directions, because a comparison check has two ways to
   be vacuous: delete a registered step from a throwaway copy of the workflow
   and confirm the check fails; add an allowlist entry naming a suite that does
   not exist and confirm the check fails. A check that only ever compares two
   empty lists passes today and after any future refactor.
5. Assert the check is itself registered in CI, not only in `run-all` — the one
   assertion whose absence would reproduce this exact defect.
6. Twin parity: the `.sh` and `.ps1` checks must return the same verdict on the
   same inputs, asserted per case rather than as a separate pass, with the ps1
   side behind a capability probe and a visible `skip -` line.
7. Re-run the baseline parse after application and put the number in
   `## Result`, together with the allowlist as it stands — the list is part of
   the result, not an implementation detail.

## Rollback-Plan

Revert the commit whose message contains `WFI-055` and remove the staged CI
steps. The pipeline returns to today's step list, which is the current
behaviour; the new check and its allowlist are removed by the same revert.

Nothing on disk needs migrating — the change alters which checks run, not any
artifact.

If the new check proves noisy in a way the allowlist makes painful (a suite
legitimately conditional, fitting neither state), the proportionate retreat is
to keep the registrations and downgrade the comparison to a warning that prints
the divergence without failing. That keeps the divergence visible, which is the
property this WFI is really buying, and gives up only the enforcement.
