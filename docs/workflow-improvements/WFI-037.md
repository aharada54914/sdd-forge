## Problem Evidence

Measured in `sdd-domain-concept-contract` on 2026-08-19, during T-004's
quality-gate cycle 3.

`plugins/sdd-review-loop/references/review-context-boundary.md` carries a
section headed "`identity_ledger_sha256`: do not re-verify". It records a real
incident -- a reviewer at sequence 402 of `epic-136-phase4-docs` re-hashed
`reports/review-context/identity-ledger.json`, found the inevitable mismatch,
and returned BLOCKED -- and prescribes the correct alternative:

> Verify the record chain instead. It is the actual integrity guarantee, it is
> verifiable after the append, and it is strictly stronger than a whole-file
> hash

followed by four numbered steps, and the claim that "A reviewer cannot do
better than this."

Three of those four steps cannot be performed by any role, because the artifact
they operate on is not authorizable as a reviewer input. Measured directly, with
the canonical ledger added to an otherwise-valid `quality:sdd-evaluator`
manifest:

```
REVIEW_CONTEXT_PATH: sdd-evaluator contains a real but role-unlisted path:
reports/review-context/identity-ledger.json
exit 1
```

`path_is_authorized` in `validate-review-context-set.sh` names the ledger in no
role branch -- not `spec-reviewer-a/b`, not `impl-reviewer-a/b`, not
`task-reviewer-a/b`, not `sdd-evaluator`, not `domain-reviewer-a/b`.

Step by step, what a reviewer can actually do:

| Prescribed step | Performable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. the record at your `sequence` exists, is **last**, and its ids match yours | **no** | requires reading the ledger |
| 2. its `previous_record_sha256` equals the previous record's `record_sha256` | **no** | requires reading the ledger |
| 3. its `record_sha256` recomputes and equals the caller-quoted value | **yes** | every input is a manifest field |
| 4. your ids appear nowhere else in the ledger | **no** | requires reading the ledger |

Step 3 verified by measurement at sequence 796:
`sha256("796|quality|sdd-evaluator|RUN-...-seq0796|SESS-...-0796|d6756a9e...")`
= `4a0c740c...`, matching the caller-quoted `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` value exactly.

What step 3 alone establishes is narrow: the caller's quoted hash is consistent
with the identity the manifest declares. It does **not** establish that the
record was appended to the ledger, that it is the chain tip, or that the
identity is unique -- which is to say it does not establish the absence proof
the ledger exists to provide.

The failure recurred today. Launching T-004's cycle 3, the orchestrator
instructed the evaluator to run the validator on its own manifest. That is
structurally guaranteed to fail after `--reserve` (the append changes the
whole-file hash and makes the identity "already persisted"), and the evaluator
correctly returned a blocking `REVIEW_CONTEXT_IDENTITY` finding having reviewed
nothing. Sequence 795 is the resulting orphan. The document that would have
prevented it is not in any evaluator's manifest either.

## Root Cause Hypothesis

The corrective guidance and the allowlist that governs what a reviewer may read
are two different surfaces, and only one of them was updated.

When the sequence-402 incident was diagnosed, the fix was written where the
diagnosis was written: into a reference document. `path_is_authorized` was never
extended to admit the artifact that document tells reviewers to inspect, and no
test compares the two. The result is guidance that reads as actionable and is
not, which is worse than silence: a reviewer that follows it hits
`REVIEW_CONTEXT_PATH`, and a reviewer that improvises reaches the ledger outside
its manifest -- exactly the unauthorized read the boundary exists to prevent.

The same shape as WFI-036: a rule and the mechanism that must implement it were
allowed to drift apart, and nothing measured the gap.

## Proposed Change

| Target File | Change Description |
|---|---|
| `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/validate-review-context-set.sh`, `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/validate-review-context-set.ps1` | Emit the chain facts the reviewer needs directly in the `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` line -- at minimum the reserved record's sequence, its `previous_record_sha256`, the pre-append tip sequence, and a uniqueness assertion for the run/session ids -- so steps 1, 2 and 4 become checkable from the caller-quoted evidence without any file read. Preferred over widening the allowlist: it adds no readable path and needs no hash-equality exemption. |
| `plugins/sdd-review-loop/references/review-context-boundary.md` | Correct the four-step procedure to say which steps a role can perform unaided, and stop asserting that a reviewer "cannot do better than this" while three of the four are unreachable. |
| `plugins/sdd-review-loop/agents/spec-reviewer-{a,b}.md`, `plugins/sdd-review-loop/agents/impl-reviewer-{a,b}.md`, `plugins/sdd-review-loop/agents/task-reviewer-{a,b}.md`, `plugins/sdd-domain/agents/domain-reviewer-{a,b}.md`, `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/agents/evaluator.md` | State plainly that the role must not run the validator against its own manifest after the reserve append, and that the caller's quoted `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` is the required evidence. The prohibition currently lives only in a reference document that is not a role input. |
| `tests/boundary-reference-authorization-parity.tests.sh` (new, with `.ps1` twin per repo convention) | Assert that every artifact the boundary reference instructs a role to read is authorized for that role by `path_is_authorized`. This is the check whose absence let the gap persist. |

Widening the allowlist to admit the ledger is the obvious alternative and is
recorded here as rejected: the ledger's hash necessarily changes at the append,
so admitting it would require exempting one path from the manifest
hash-equality rule -- the single substantive integrity check -- to fix a
documentation defect.

## Expected Effect

A reviewer can verify its own launch identity to the strength the boundary
reference claims, using evidence it is actually given. Today it can verify one
of four properties and must take the other three on the launching agent's word,
while a document it cannot read tells it otherwise.

Measured effect: reduce identity-boundary blocks — roles blocking on their own
launch identity rather than on the work under review
(`metrics.reviewer_identity_boundary_blocks`) — from 2 measured occurrences in
the baseline period to 0 across the next 10 reviewer or evaluator launches, and
raise the launch-identity properties a role can verify unaided from 1 of 4 to
4 of 4.

## Verification Plan

For each of the next ten reviewer or evaluator launches, record whether the role
verified its launch identity, which of the four properties it verified, and
whether it blocked on identity rather than on the work. The WFI is verified when
ten consecutive launches verify all four from caller-supplied evidence and none
blocks on identity.

One control is required, because this WFI moves an integrity property from a
file read to a caller-supplied line, and a caller-supplied assertion is worth
nothing if it cannot be falsified: construct a launch whose quoted chain facts
disagree with the ledger the validator actually wrote, and confirm the role
rejects it. If a fabricated `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` line is indistinguishable from a
genuine one, the change has relocated the trust rather than preserved it, and
the allowlist route should be reconsidered despite its cost.

## Rollback-Plan

Revert the commit whose message contains `WFI-037`. That restores the
`REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` line to its bare `OK <record-hash>` form, restores the
boundary reference's four-step wording, removes the added role text, and removes
the parity test. No manifest, evidence bundle or ledger record changes shape:
the ledger is not touched by either the change or its rollback, and manifests
already written stay valid because the change only adds fields to a validator's
stdout.

## Result

**Applied 2026-08-22** (commit whose message contains `WFI-037`). All four
Proposed Change rows landed:

- **Validator twins.** Both `validate-review-context-set.{sh,ps1}` now emit
  `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK <record_sha256> sequence=<n>
  previous_record_sha256=<hash|-> pre_append_tip_sequence=<n|->
  identity_unique=yes` — each fact proven by a fail-closed check before the
  line prints. `pre_append_tip_sequence` is `-` on the verification of an
  already-persisted identity, where tip position is meaningless by the
  validator's own design comment. The uniqueness assertion required one new
  check: the persisted-verification branch now proves the run/session pair
  appears exactly once (the reserve branch already proved it via the
  collision checks). The line's first two tokens are unchanged, so every
  existing consumer that requires or captures `REVIEW_CONTEXT_OK` still
  matches; `review-context-boundary.tests.sh`'s TEST-RCB-001..010
  functional cases and `review-contract-foundation.tests.sh` pass
  unchanged.
- **Boundary reference.** The four-step procedure is rewritten to be
  performable by its audience: every step names its evidence source
  (caller-quoted line, plus the role's own manifest), the impossibility of
  a ledger read is stated as the reason, and the "cannot do better"
  claim is replaced by what the evidence actually establishes — including
  the falsifiability argument the Verification Plan's control demands: a
  fabricated OK line fails the recompute arithmetically or describes a
  record the next validator run's unconditional chain walk refuses.
- **Role documents.** All nine (spec/impl/task reviewer a+b, domain
  reviewer a+b, evaluator) carry one byte-identical paragraph prohibiting
  self-validation after the reserve append and naming the caller-quoted
  line as the required evidence.
- **Parity test.** `tests/boundary-reference-authorization-parity.tests.sh`
  (+ `.ps1` twin, both registered in run-all and in the CI matrix) pins the
  reconciled state from both sides: every verification step names an
  evidence source a role actually has; `path_is_authorized` /
  `Test-AuthorizedPath` still refuse the ledger (the rejected
  widening alternative cannot creep back); both twins emit identical
  chain-fact keys; the nine role paragraphs stay byte-identical. 19
  passed, 0 failed.
- Also fixed in passing: `tests/review-context-boundary.tests.sh`'s
  citation-drift assertion still pointed at `impl-review-precheck.sh` for a
  message the #325 consolidation moved into
  `lib/review-precheck-common.sh` — the suite (not run in CI) had been
  failing on main since that merge; it now points at the lib and passes
  13/13.
