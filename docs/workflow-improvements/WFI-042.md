## Problem Evidence

One field, two grammars, and a third behaviour between them — measured on
2026-08-22 during the plugin code-quality audit (report
`reports/notes/plugin-code-quality-audit-2026-08-21.md`, deferred item 2).

The task approval field is validated by two deterministic checkers that
disagree about what an annotated approval is:

- `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/check-task-state.sh:85-87` (and its `.ps1`
  twin) accept `Approved (<any non-empty annotation>)`.
- `plugins/sdd-lite/scripts/check-task-state-lite.sh:57-59` (and its `.ps1`
  twin) require `Approved (<id> <ISO8601, seconds, Z>)` and reject everything
  else.

The strict regex ALSO lives inside the full checker itself: `approver_id()`
(`check-task-state.sh:46-54`) extracts a named approver only from the strict
form, and the critical-Done second-approval check (`:155-165`) fails when it
returns empty. So under the full checker a loose annotation is simultaneously
(a) a valid approval for ordinary tasks and (b) an invalid approval for
critical-Done tasks — two verdicts about the same line, produced by the same
script, with a diagnostic that reports the second as a missing named approver
rather than as a grammar problem.

Corpus measurement (all committed `specs/*/tasks.md`, 2026-08-22): 107 bare
approvals, 4 drafts, ~170 strict-form annotated approvals — and **8 committed
human approval lines that match neither grammar exactly**:

- 4 × `Approved (human 2026-08-17T03:35Z)` — minutes precision, no seconds;
- 4 × `Approved (sudo 2026-07-13T05:53:16.481Z)` (and three siblings) —
  fractional seconds.

All 8 pass the full checker (any annotation) and would fail the lite checker
and `approver_id()`'s strict pattern. They are human-made records in completed
epics: retroactively rewriting them is not an agent decision, and silently
grandfathering them is how the split survives.

## Why-Why Analysis

| # | Why | Because | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why can one approval line be simultaneously valid and invalid across the workflow's own checkers? | The full checker's validity test accepts any non-empty annotation while the lite checker and the full checker's own approver-extraction accept only the `<id> <ISO8601>` form — three sites, two grammars, one field. | `check-task-state.sh:85-87` vs `check-task-state-lite.sh:57-59` vs `check-task-state.sh:46-54` |
| 2 | Why do the sites disagree? | Each grammar was written at the moment its feature needed it: the loose form when annotations were free-text provenance notes, the strict form when the named-approver requirement (critical-Done second approval) and the lite profile were added — and the older site was never reconciled. | git history of the two checkers; the strict regex appears only in the later-added `approver_id()` and lite paths (hypothesis for the ordering, consistent with blame) |
| 3 | Why was the older site not reconciled when the strict form arrived? | The strict requirement was implemented as a NEW extraction function beside the old validity test instead of replacing it, so both continued to pass CI — nothing compares the two checkers' or the two paths' accepted languages. | `approver_id()` coexisting with the loose conditional in the same file; no grammar-parity fixture in `tests/` before this WFI |
| 4 | Why does nothing compare them? | The two checkers live in different plugins (full: quality-loop; lite: sdd-lite) with deliberately independent packaging, and no cross-plugin parity suite existed for shared vocabulary — the same mechanism class WFI-038 recorded ("two surfaces must agree and nothing asserts it"). | plugin layout under `plugins/`; `tests/task-lifecycle-enum-parity.tests.sh` (added with this WFI's evidence) is the first cross-checker vocabulary assertion |

The chain terminates at a controllable mechanism — a stricter grammar added
beside, not into, the existing validity test, with no cross-surface assertion —
which is what `## Proposed Change` acts on.

## Root Cause Hypothesis

The strict named-approver grammar was introduced for new requirements
(critical-Done second approvals; the lite profile) as additional code paths
rather than as a revision of the existing validity grammar. Because the loose
validity test kept passing everything the strict paths needed plus more,
no test ever failed, and the three sites' accepted languages drifted apart
invisibly. The 8 non-conforming committed lines are the residue: humans wrote
annotations the loose site accepted and no site canonicalized.

## Proposed Change

| Target File | Change Description |
|---|---|
| `plugins/sdd-quality-loop/scripts/check-task-state.sh` and `.ps1` twin | Replace the loose annotated-approval validity test with the strict `<id> <ISO8601>` grammar already used by `approver_id()`, so validity and approver-extraction accept the same language and the critical-Done diagnostic can no longer disagree with the validity verdict. Bare `Approved` and `Draft` unchanged. |
| `plugins/sdd-lite/scripts/check-task-state-lite.sh` and `.ps1` twin | No grammar change — the strict form is already enforced; listed for blast-radius visibility and covered by the new parity fixtures. |
| `specs/*/tasks.md` (8 lines, enumerated in `## Problem Evidence`) | Human migration: re-annotate the 8 non-conforming lines to the strict form (add `:00` seconds / drop fractional seconds), performed or explicitly delegated by a human since these are human approval records. The agent stages a patch; a human applies it. |
| `tests/task-state-grammar-parity.tests.sh` (new) | Fixture-driven parity: both checkers (sh legs; ps1 legs in CI) must agree on a shared accept/reject fixture set — bare, strict-annotated, minutes-precision, fractional-seconds, empty-annotation, free-text — and `approver_id()` must extract a name from every form the validity test accepts as annotated. |

## Expected Effect

The count of approval-grammar disagreements between the deterministic
checkers — lines one checker accepts and the other (or the same checker's
approver-extraction) rejects — drops from 8 committed instances plus one
structural class to 0, and stays 0 because the parity fixtures fail on the
next divergence.

## Result

**Applied 2026-08-22** (commit whose message contains `WFI-042`), with one
blast-radius addition discovered at implementation:

- `check-task-state.{sh,ps1}`: validity and gate checks now use the strict
  grammar `approver_id()` / `Get-ApproverId` extract from. The ps1 leg uses
  `-match` (not `-cmatch`), matching the lite twin's identical sites so the
  two ps1 legs of the parity fixtures agree.
- **Addition — `mcp/sdd-forge-mcp/src/parsers/task-validation.ts`**: the MCP
  server's task parser implemented the same loose annotation pattern and is
  bound to `check-task-state.sh` by golden shell-equivalence tests
  (`tests/golden/task-state-golden.test.ts`), so it cannot stay loose while
  the shell tightens. Its `APPROVED_ANNOTATION_PATTERN` now carries the
  strict grammar (capture group preserved for the annotation echo). Its
  loose-acceptance unit test was flipped into a strict-accept +
  loose-reject pair. MCP suite: 240 passed, 0 failed — the golden
  comparisons ran live against the updated shell checker. The 8 golden
  features carry no non-conforming lines, so recorded fixtures needed no
  re-recording.
- `tests/task-state-grammar-parity.tests.sh` (new, registered in run-all):
  seven accept/reject fixtures across full+lite sh legs (ps1 legs behind a
  pwsh probe with a visible skip line) plus a four-fixture
  extraction-agreement leg on the critical-Done path. **Discriminating
  order verified** (Verification Plan item 1): pointed at the pre-change
  checker via its env override, the suite fails 6 cases — the
  minutes-precision, fractional-seconds and free-text fixtures on both
  legs, reproducing the committed 8-line class — and passes 11/11 after.
- `tests/gates.tests.sh` C-05.2: expectation flipped — the relaxed format
  (#35.3) is retired; gates suite 131 passed, 0 failed.
  `task-lifecycle-enum-parity` stays green (item 3): 7 passed.
- **Migration staged, not applied** (`## Proposed Change` row 3): the 8
  human approval lines are rewritten by
  `reports/notes/wfi-042-approval-line-migration.patch` (cover note beside
  it, `git apply` verified clean). They are human records in completed
  epics; a human applies the patch. CI does not gate on those two epics'
  tasks.md, so green does not depend on when that happens.

**Migration applied 2026-08-23 under explicit human delegation** (the
operator's direct instruction to perform it — the delegation `## Proposed
Change` row 3 provides for). The staged patch applied cleanly; Verification
Plan item 2's corpus sweep now reads **zero** non-conforming lines across
all committed `specs/*/tasks.md`, and the grammar-parity (11), lifecycle
enum-parity (7) and gates (131) suites stay green over the migrated
corpus. The staged patch and cover note remain in `reports/notes/` as the
record of what was rewritten.

**Amendment (2026-08-23, external review on PR #336).** The ps1 legs'
`-match` was case-insensitive, so a mis-cased annotation (`approved (alice
…z)`) passed validity only on PowerShell hosts — violating both the
one-grammar claim and AGENTS.md's case-sensitivity sweep rule (WFI-012).
All annotated-approval sites in `check-task-state.ps1` (validity, gating,
`Get-ApproverId`) and `check-task-state-lite.ps1` (validity, gating) now
use `-cmatch`, and the parity suite gains the mandatory mis-cased negative
fixture (12 passed, 0 failed; ps1 legs verify in CI).
