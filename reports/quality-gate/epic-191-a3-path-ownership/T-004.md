## Findings

Critical: 1. Major: 2. Minor: 2.

### Critical 1 — the apply broke the repository's central deterministic gate suite

This is the finding nobody had measured, and it was not in the briefing.

Adding `check-component-coverage` to the live `high`/`critical` tier-minimum set
makes every pre-existing high-risk and critical contract in this repository
invalid, because none of them carries the new required check. `tests/gates.tests.sh`
goes from 126 passed / 0 failed to **114 passed / 12 failed**.

Independently reproduced for this report, not taken from the evaluator:

- The suite reports `PASS: 114` / `FAIL: 12`, total still 126, so exactly twelve
  assertions flipped.
- The twelve are T-003.7, T-003.8, T-012.7, T-004.3, T-004.7, T-006.3b,
  T-007a.1d, T-007a.5, T-007a.9, CM.1, CM.3 and CM.4 — every one a
  `high`/`critical` "should pass" case.
- Four print the cause verbatim:
  `risk high requires check 'check-component-coverage' present and required:true (missing)`.
  The other eight are downstream: evidence-bundle and cross-model paths that
  build a high or critical contract internally.
- Causation confirmed by diffing the tier set across the apply commit. At
  `c1db8b57~1`, `RISK_TIERS["high"]` and `["critical"]` end at
  `requirement-traceability`; at HEAD both carry `check-component-coverage`.
  Nothing else in that commit touches contract validation.

Three things make this blocking rather than expected:

1. `tasks.md:778` declares `Breaking API: no`, arguing the `check-contract`
   edits "narrow acceptance rather than loosening it". Narrowing acceptance is
   exactly what breaks every contract written under the old set. The task
   shipped no migration for existing contracts, fixtures, or the
   evidence-bundle generator.
2. The report's Test Evidence claims `bash tests/gates.tests.sh` "reconfirmed
   126 passed / 0 failed, unchanged". That was true pre-apply and is false now.
3. The report's own Unresolved Items #1 made a falsifiable post-apply
   prediction — "re-run `bash tests/gates.tests.sh` (T-003.7 should continue to
   pass — it does not itself exercise `check-component-coverage`)". T-003.7 is
   the first of the twelve failures. The prediction was wrong in the specific
   case it named.

Nothing in the frozen `design.md` or `tasks.md` anticipates or accepts this.
`design.md:843-858`'s staged-rollout argument covers only the Gate's own exit
code, and `:853` explicitly notes that `check-contract`'s tier-minimum set has
no capability-state axis — so there is no `disabled-legacy` escape for contracts
the way there is for the Gate.

This needs a decision, not just a patch. Either existing high/critical contracts
gain the check, or the tier-minimum entry becomes conditional on the capability
state, or the rollout is staged some third way. It is out of an evaluator's
remit to choose, and it is out of this task's remit to choose silently.

### Major 1 — the suite is anti-correlated with the required state

`tests/check-component-coverage.tests.sh:351-356, 357-379, 428-436` and the ps1
equivalents. Measured: bash 38 passed / 3 failed, pwsh 38 passed / 2 failed.
Done-When 2, 3 and 4 all say "TEST-… pass", so they are literally unmet, and the
suite is registered in `tests/run-all.sh:101` and `run-all.ps1:60`.

The disclosure in the report is honest and the line numbers are right, but the
evaluator found the problem is worse than "three stale assertions". The suite is
inverted with respect to the criterion it is supposed to guard:

- TEST-036.4 raises `"{n} unexpectedly already live"` for each of the three
  suffixes — it fails *because* the registration AC-036(d) demands is present.
- TEST-036.3 asserts the live `check-contract` must NOT register the check.
- TEST-055.1 asserts the live `check-contract` must ACCEPT tampered evidence.

Meanwhile TEST-035.1, TEST-036.5, TEST-055.2/.3 and the whole pwsh TEST-055
assert only against `reports/implementation/<feature>/drafts/`, which this report
itself now calls byte-identical superseded leftovers with no enforcement role.

The net effect is the sharp end: if the live protected files were reverted
tomorrow, TEST-035.1 would stay green and the other three would turn green. The
suite would report 41/0 with the enforcement chain gone.

TEST-055.1's failure text is itself proof the live chain now works:
`producer.sha256 (000…0) does not match the live on-disk check-component-coverage.py (f2ec1f5b…)`.

The corrections this report proposed for TEST-036.3 and TEST-055.1 — invert to
the positive, anchored structurally to the live set literal — are right and
strictly stronger. Two gaps in that proposal: TEST-036.4's replacement was left
"re-express against a stable one", which is under-specified, and the proposal
does not notice that TEST-035.1 stays draft-anchored even after the other fixes.

### Major 2 — TEST-054.4 is tautological and was presented as closing a Major

`tests/check-component-coverage.tests.sh:246-253`. Verified line-level for this
report: `tampered_producer` is produced by
`jq '.producer.sha256 = "000…0"' | jq -r '.producer.sha256'`, so it is the
literal constant jq just wrote. The guarded comparison
`[ "$tampered_producer" != "$real_sha" ]` cannot fail short of a sha256 preimage
of all zeros. The assertion's own success message reads "the self-check is not
tautological", which is exactly what it is.

This matters because Quality-Gate Remediation §5 presents TEST-054.4 as closing
cycle 1's Major on AC-054's negative clause. AC-054
(`requirements.md:1133-1134`) requires that "a fixture with a mismatched or
missing `producer.sha256` is rejected by the suite's own self-check". No such
rejection is exercised anywhere in the suite. TEST-055.2 does cover rejection,
but that is AC-055, and it runs against the draft copy.

## Decision

T-004 stays `Status: Implementation Complete`. One Critical and two Majors block
Done.

The ordering matters. Major 1 should not be fixed before Critical 1 is decided,
because how the twelve `gates.tests.sh` failures are resolved determines what the
corrected TEST-036.3 and TEST-055.1 should assert. If the tier-minimum entry
becomes conditional on the capability state, the positive assertions the report
proposes would themselves be wrong.

Critical 1 is not agent-decidable. It is a rollout question about a change that
is already live in the working tree and already breaking the repository's
enforcement suite, and `tasks.md`'s frozen `Breaking API: no` is now demonstrably
false. It should go to a human on a review ticket.
